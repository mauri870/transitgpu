# Changes

## Unreleased

### Added

- `transitgpu.detrend.biweight(time, flux, window, mask=None)` returns the trend of wotan's biweight filter, with wotan's windows, its mask and its stopping step. On a CUDA or ROCm device where Triton can be imported a kernel with one program for each point computes it, reading each window from the flux array; elsewhere torch operations on a matrix of windows do, on the CPU too. The tests compare both with wotan 1.10, and `benchmarks/bench_detrend.py` times them against it on light curves of 30 minutes to 20 seconds of cadence.
- `transitgpu.detrend.biweight_many(sectors, window, masks=None)` returns the trends of a list of (time, flux) light curves from one sequence of launches; each equals the trend of `biweight` of that light curve alone.
- The kernel computes the trend of windows padded to at most `detrend.KERNEL_WIDTH_LIMIT` points; a wider window runs the torch version on the same device. On the CPU the trend is wotan's own when wotan can be imported, which is faster there than the torch version at 2-minute cadence (`benchmarks/bench_detrend.py`). `method="kernel"`, `"torch"` or `"wotan"` on `biweight` and `biweight_many` chooses the path.

### Changed

- `search`, `search_many`, `box_search(...).power`, `bls_tls_hybrid` and the `gpu` and `gpu-binned` backends of `transitgpu.tls` run on the CPU when torch finds no GPU or when `device="cpu"` is given, and no longer raise a `RuntimeError`. The CPU is slower than a GPU: TLS's exact statistic of 3,000 points from 1 to 6 days takes 1.6 s with `gpu` on 16 threads against 0.16 s with TLS's `fused`, and `gpu-binned` takes 0.18 s. `gpu-pl` has only Triton kernels and raises on a CPU with a message that names `gpu` and `gpu-binned`. Asking for `device="cuda"` where torch finds no GPU still raises, with the link to the torch build for the driver.
- The fold, the scoring and the update of the best boxes are compiled by torch.compile on the CPU as well as on a GPU. On a CPU, a second search of 4,000 points from 1 to 6 days takes 0.02 s and not 0.43 s, after a first call of about 10 s that compiles (about 2 s from torch's compile cache; AMD Ryzen 9 9950X3D, torch 2.14.1+cpu). Where torch cannot compile, for example without a C++ compiler, a warning is shown once and the eager passes are used, as on a GPU.

### Known limits

- The compile of the detrend kernel grows about four times for each doubling of the padded window, because the kernel sorts the whole window in registers; above `detrend.KERNEL_WIDTH_LIMIT` the torch version is used instead. A sort that does not unroll the window would let the kernel take wider windows.

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-05)

### Added

- `radius_limits` and `mass_limits`, pairs (minimum, maximum) in solar units, on `duration_fractions`, `search` and `search_many` replace TLS's stellar limits for the durations of the grid. A smaller minimum radius lowers the shortest duration in proportion and adds shorter durations and more phase bins at every period; every duration of the default grid stays in the lowered one, and a search without the arguments is bit for bit the search as it was.
- `bumps=True` on `box_search(...).power`, `search` and `search_many` scores the windows whose mean is above the whole's from the same fold, sums and grid as the dips. Each result then has a `bump` entry with the fields of a result, equal to the result of a search of the negated flux, and the results of `power` a `bump` attribute; the other fields are those of a search without `bumps`.

### Changed

- `depth_ppm` and `depth_mean_ppm` are NaN when the absolute mean level of the flux is under the standard deviation of the flux, for example a flux with its mean subtracted, which gave a depth of infinity or of the wrong sign. The depth is relative to the mean level, as a flux flattened and normalized to 1 has it; the docstring says so. The period, the SDE and the SNR do not depend on the level.
- `search_many` and `search` raise a `ValueError` that names the argument for `duration_step` that is not finite or not above 1, `minimum_bins` under 1, `batch` under 1 (None is the default), periods that are not finite and above 0, fewer than two distinct periods (the SDE is a z-score over the periods), and a `duration_hours` window that leaves no duration to score at any period. The last was a search that found nothing, with an SDE of 0.
- `search_many` warns once for a call when some of the durations it scores are shorter than half a phase bin of their period. The bins of a period stop at 4,096, so at 100 days a bin is 35 minutes and a lowered floor of `radius_limits` or `mass_limits` below half a bin was scored as one bin without a word. The docstring states the cap. The default limits give no such duration at any period up to 1,000 days.
- The Triton kernels of `transitgpu.core` are defined when the module is imported, as those of `transitgpu.tls` already were. A search that builds a kernel after a sync of the environment has replaced the package's files no longer reads the new file with the line numbers of the code it imported, which raised a `CompilationError`; it uses the source it imported. Without Triton, `import transitgpu` no longer warns at the first search that the fused accumulation is unavailable (the fold table and the scatters are used as before), and an error other than `ImportError` raised by Triton at import now stops the import instead of being caught. Importing the package takes about 0.08 s longer.
- A launch of the one-pass accumulation counts the fluxes of its call. The kernel visits every point of its block once for every flux, so a call of 8 fluxes launched 13 ms at 236,736 points and 23 ms at 2.5 million points against 5 ms for one flux; `one_pass_batch(points, fluxes)` now takes fewer periods a launch for a call of several fluxes (a flux after the first counts 0.4 of the first, in 140 million period-point pairs), and a call of one flux takes the periods it took. The result is the same to the bit; a search of several fluxes over a stack of 150,000 points or more makes more, shorter launches.
- On ROCm, a search of one flux accumulates on the HIP kernel, as a search of several fluxes with shared weights already did. The sums and counts are the same integers as the Triton kernel's, and a search of one flux takes 16% less time at 20,000 points and 34% less at 100,000 (`benchmarks/results.json`, row `gpu`, Radeon RX 7900 XTX: 0.267 and 0.505 s with 0.1.0 in the session of 2026-10-26, 0.223 and 0.333 s with 0.2.0 in the session of 2026-11-05).
- `search_many` without `boxes=True` keeps the fields of the best box (sum, start, width and number of points) at the best period of each flux and each cut and not at every trial period, as the SDE needs only the statistic of every period. A search of 10 fluxes over 8,060 periods holds 1.36 MB less at its peak (numpy allocations, `tracemalloc`; 4 of the 5 float64 or int64 arrays of fluxes by periods), and every field of every result is equal to that of the search with `boxes=True`, bit for bit.
- `search_many` with one flux and a matrix of weights, one row for each result, searches the flux once for each row from one fold of the times, and each result is that of a search of the flux with that row. A weight of zero now masks its point, which is dropped as a point with no flux is; before it was refused, and a negative or non-finite weight still is.
  A batch of five vectors takes 0.69 to 0.81 of the time of five separate searches (20,000 to 200,000 points) and not the time of one: each vector pays its own accumulation, 1,159 ms of the 1,531 ms of kernel time of five vectors at 100,000 points against 247 of 323 ms for one, because the weights differ and the one-pass kernels add the flux and the count of every point for every vector. The HIP kernel, which needs one set of weights, bounds a kernel that took them per vector at 0.40 of the batch's time at 100,000 points; it is not built, since a search with several sets of weights is rare next to the searches of many fluxes with one set, which share the fold already.
- The best boxes of a batch of periods are updated by one compiled function and not by about twenty eager passes: a search launches about half the kernels and takes 6% less time at 100,000 points; the numbers are the same to the bit.
- torch's limit of compiled variants of a function is raised to 64 when it is lower, and never lowered. At torch's default of 8, a process that used more combinations of options than that ran the scorer eagerly for the rest of its life, which changes the float32 statistic in its seventh digit.

### Fixed

- The HIP accumulation is built from the text of `accumulate.hip` as it was when `transitgpu.hip` was imported, written to a file named by its hash in the build directory. The file was read from its path at the first build, which a process that survives a sync of the environment reaches after the package's files were replaced, as the Triton kernels were fixed to read their source at import.
- The C++ entry point of the HIP accumulation checks its arguments: every tensor on one device and contiguous, the dtypes, 1 to 4,096 bins, as many points in `quantized` and `weight` as in `t`, and the sizes of the sums and counts. It makes the device of `t` current for the launch, and it raises when the launch reports an error, which it did not look at before. `hip.accumulate_many` makes the time axis contiguous.
- On ROCm, the HIP accumulation of `search_many` is launched with the device of the time axis as the current device, as the Triton kernel already was. With `device='cuda:1'` on a host of two GPUs it launched on the stream of device 0 over the pointers of device 1. Tested by the structure of the call, since one GPU was available: two devices were not tested.
- The exact search (`transitgpu.tls`, backend `gpu`) of a single trial period raised `ValueError: At least one stride in the given numpy array is negative`: the periods were sorted into descending order through a reversed view, and `np.ascontiguousarray` returns a view of one element unchanged. The periods are copied.
- Exact mode (`transitgpu.tls`, backend `gpu`) returned wrong chi squares for part of the periods of a batch when the batch held more than 33,554,432 starts that passed the bounds of the screening. The correlations of those starts were launched at once, and a launch of that size is not dispatched whole; millions of them got other values, and the best period of a search could move. It needs about 524,000 such starts for each period at a batch of 64 periods, so a light curve with very many of them, or a batch of more than 256 periods chosen by the caller, could reach it. The checks of this package never reached it at the default batch. The correlations are now launched in groups of at most 8,388,608 starts, and a test launches 33,555,432 starts and compares them with a small launch.
- On CPU-only torch, importing the package's HIP helper no longer raises when it asks for the architecture of a device.

### Changed

- The exact search batches up to 256 periods, by the free device memory, instead of 64 for long light curves; the chi squares are the same as at 64. A batch whose mask of starts would pass 2^30 entries (periods times starts) is refused with an error that names both numbers.
