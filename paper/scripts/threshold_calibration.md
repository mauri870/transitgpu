# Calibration of the block-shuffle threshold: question, method and rules

Written before any light curve of the calibration is searched.

## Question

The paper sets a threshold per light curve as the largest SDE of 48 copies of it, shuffled in blocks of one day within each sector, and argues that a noise-only light curve then exceeds its threshold in 1 search in 49 (2.04 percent). The argument needs the copies and the light curve to be exchangeable. Is the share of noise-only light curves whose best peak exceeds their own threshold 1/49, for each class of noise? How much of the injected planets does the search find above that threshold, and above the threshold of an alternative null, the 48 shuffles of the inverted light curve?

## Expectation

Argued, not measured. White noise is exchangeable under any permutation of the points, so the share is 1/49 for white noise. A shuffle in blocks of one day keeps the correlation inside a block and removes it between blocks; noise correlated over more than a day, or a variation that is coherent over several days, gives the original light curve peaks of the box search at long periods that its copies do not have, so the share is above 1/49 for correlated and variable light curves, and the more so the longer the correlation time. If the correlation time is under a day the share stays near 1/49. The injected planets are found above the threshold less often when the shuffles of the planet's own light curve keep some of its transits and so raise the threshold. What would show this wrong: a share of white noise outside its interval around 1/49 (the procedure or the code is at fault), or a share of the correlated and variable classes inside it (the day block is long enough).

## Method

Light curves. The generator of `validation/generator.py`, shared with the injection-recovery evaluation: one sector of 27.4 d at 2-minute cadence (19,740 points), the classes `white` (1000 ppm), `red` (700 ppm white and an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process of 500 ppm with a correlation time of 0.3 d) and `variable` (700 ppm white and a rotation sinusoid of 3000 ppm with a 40 percent first harmonic, at a period log-uniform from 0.8 to 10 d), the last two flattened with the 3-day biweight of `generator.flatten` before anything else is done to them, and the 12 real quiet stars of `validation/stars.json` (one sector each, fetched by `validation/fetch.py`, flattened as the generator's classes are). The seed of the curve of class c (0 white, 1 red, 2 variable) and index i is 9,000,000 + 100,000 c + i, a range that the injection-recovery evaluation does not use. A curve is the same whichever call builds it.

Search. The box search of `transitgpu.search_many` on the period grid of TLS from 0.5 to 14 d for the baseline of the light curve, the light curve and its 48 shuffles in one call (`paper/scripts/injection.py` `block_shuffles`, blocks of 1 d within each sector, copy i seeded with the seed of the curve plus i). The SDE is the one the search returns (the z-score of the best statistic of each period over the grid of that light curve, so each copy has its own).

Inverted null. The flux is reflected about its median, `2 * median - flux`, and shuffled the same way with the same seeds; the threshold is the largest SDE of its 48 copies. The box search looks for dips, so a planet becomes a bump in this null and does not enter its threshold.

Injection. The planets of R10, put into the first curves of each class, so that the same noise is searched with and without a planet. A planet is right when its period is within 1 percent and its epoch within half its duration (`generator.classify`) and found when it is right and the best peak is at or above the threshold.

## Rules

R1. Null. For each synthetic class, N noise-only light curves, N written under Cost; the 12 real stars are 12 curves, too few for a share to be resolved (a share of 0 of 12 has an upper end near 24 percent), so they are reported one by one under R9. The share of curves whose best SDE is strictly above the largest SDE of their 48 shuffles, with its Wilson 95 percent interval. Ties are counted and reported; none is expected.

R2. A class is calibrated when its interval contains 1/49 and not calibrated when it does not. A class that is not calibrated is reported with its share and its interval, and the paper presents the threshold for that class as a procedure whose rate is that share, not 1/49.

R3. The calibration curve. For k from 1 to 10, the share of the curves whose best SDE is above the k-th largest of their 48 shuffles, against k/49, with Wilson intervals; a rank curve above the line k/49 shows where the tail is off, not only at the first rank.

R4. The tail resolution. With 48 copies the threshold of one light curve is the maximum of 48 draws, so the false-alarm probability of that threshold for that light curve is Beta(1, 48) distributed with mean 1/49. Its median, 90th and 99th percentiles are computed and stated beside the shares (computed, not measured).

R5. Alternative null. The same share and interval for the inverted null (R1, R2), and the share of the curves in which the two thresholds differ by more than 10 percent of the shuffle threshold.

R6. Recovery. For each class, M injections in the first M curves of the class (R10), M written under Cost. For each: the SDE of the best peak, whether it is right, the thresholds of the planet's own shuffles and inverted shuffles. The share of the planets found above each threshold, by the injected S/N used by the injection-recovery evaluation, with Wilson intervals; and the share of the planets whose own shuffle threshold is above the threshold of the same noise without the planet, with the median ratio of the two (the effect of a planet on its own null).

R7. Nothing is tuned: the block (1 d), the number of shuffles (48), the grid, the tolerances (1 percent, half a duration) and the classes are those above. A change after a result is read is a new plan with its own numbers.

R8. A run resumes from its rows file and skips a finished curve before it generates, loads or searches anything.

R9. Real stars. Each real star is searched with 199 shuffles in place of 48; the table lists the rank of its best SDE among the 199 (the share of the shuffles below it), which is uniform on 0 to 1 for an exchangeable star. The ranks are listed and tested against uniform with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic; the test has little power with 12 stars and the paper says so.

R10. The planets of the recovery. The grid of the injection-recovery evaluation: periods 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.4 and 12.8 d and target S/N 4, 6, 8, 10, 14 and 20 (S/N is sqrt(sum of (1 - model)^2) / sigma over the cadences, `generator.signal_to_noise`, with the white noise sigma of the class), 30 cells. Curve i of a class carries cell i mod 30 (periods vary slowest), with the impact parameter uniform from 0 to 0.9 and the epoch fraction uniform, drawn from the seed of the curve; the radius ratio is `generator.with_signal_to_noise`. The planet is injected in the flux before flattening, as a pipeline sees it. `generator.classify` gives right, harmonic or wrong; a planet is found when it is right and its SDE is above the threshold, and a harmonic is counted apart, never as found. The planet of curve i is searched on the noise of null curve i, so that the threshold of the noise alone is known for the same noise (R6).

R11. Amendment of R10 (2026-10-24, before any red or variable injection is rerun and before any row of a variable injection exists). In the red and variable classes the correlated noise does not average down over a transit, so the S/N against the white sigma is not the S/N of the planet (the injection-recovery evaluation measured recovery near zero at every nominal S/N up to 20 in these classes). For these two classes the target S/N is against the noise of the quiet light curve at the duration of the transit, after Pont, Zucker and Queloz (2006, MNRAS 373, 231): `generator.effective_sigma(quiet, duration)`, with `quiet` the flattened noise-only flux of the same curve (the null curve), and the radius ratio solved twice because the duration depends on it. The white class is unchanged. The 1,500 red injections run with the white sigma are kept in `first-attempt-red-injection.jsonl`, unread, and reported as the first attempt; they are not part of the result. The sigma used is stored in each row (`sigma`), and the nominal and the effective S/N are not compared again.

R12. Rotation in the variable null (written 2026-10-24 after the rows were analysed, before any crossing is counted against the rotation period). The variable class contains a real periodic signal, the rotation of the generator, which a shuffle in blocks of one day destroys. The rotation period of curve i is the period that the generator draws for the seed of the curve (the draw that follows the white-noise draw of the same generator). A crossing of a variable curve (best SDE strictly above the largest shuffle, R1) is explained by rotation when the best period of the curve is within 1 percent of r times the rotation period, for r in 1/3, 1/2, 1, 2 and 3. Counted: the crossings explained by rotation and those that are not, with Wilson intervals; the same count among the curves that do not cross (the share of curves whose best period is near the rotation period without crossing), which is the control; and the share of the period grid that the windows cover (the chance of a best period in them). If most crossings are explained by rotation, the paper says that the shuffle null treats a real periodic signal as a signal; the share of the crossings that are not explained is the rate that remains for noise.

R13. The red check (written 2026-10-24 before any run of it). The null of the red class is conservative: 16 of 2,000 curves cross (0.80 percent), and the best peak of the original ranks below its own shuffles (the mean share of the 48 shuffles below it is 0.410 over the 2,000 curves and 0.420 over the first 200, standard errors 0.006 and 0.021). The argued mechanism is that a shuffle in blocks of one day of the flattened flux puts steps at the joints of the blocks, which the filter had removed from the original, and the steps raise the SDE of the copies. If that is the mechanism, copies made as the filter would see them, by shuffling the unflattened flux in blocks and flattening each copy, have no steps at the joints that the filter leaves, so the mean share of the copies below the original rises toward 0.5 and the share of crossings toward 1/49. Measured on the first 200 red curves (the 3-day biweight on each of 48 shuffled copies of the unflattened flux, then the same search as R1): (a) the mean share of the copies below the original, with its standard error; (b) the crossings and their Wilson interval, and the median ratio of the largest copy's SDE to the largest of the flatten-then-shuffle copies of the same curve. The mechanism is supported when (a) rises by at least 3 standard errors of the difference from 0.420 and (b) the median ratio is below 1. It is not supported when (a) stays within 2 standard errors of 0.420; then the cause is not the steps at the joints and is not known. Between 2 and 3 standard errors the check does not decide. Cost: the filter is the cost, 0.83 s a copy, so 200 curves with 48 copies are 8,000 s of one process (about 22 minutes on 6 workers) and under a minute of device time (49 searches a curve, 0.16 s). The power of 200 curves for the share of crossings is low (4 crossings are expected at 1/49, and the interval of 4 of 200 is 0.8 to 5 percent), which is why (a) decides.

R14. The list of real stars (written 2026-10-24, before the rows of the current list exist). The first 12 real-star rows (25081575, 32069592, 38696814, 154926789, 156198712, 177237464, 198161846, 198390801, 219852735, 233722148, 382199165, 441717318) were made from the first version of `validation/stars.json` (commit e833f703). The injection-recovery evaluation then changed the list to 12 stars chosen by type (commit 472db8bc), and that list is the one of the repository. R9 is run again, unchanged, on the current list; its rows are the result of this section, and the rows of the first list are reported beside it as a first list, not dropped. The two lists share 4 stars, whose rows are the same (the shuffles are seeded with the TIC); the pooled test counts each of the distinct stars once. Nothing in R9 changes: 199 shuffles, the rank share, the Kolmogorov statistic.

R13 status (2026-10-24). Not run: the scope of the work was frozen before the check, and the cause of the conservative threshold of the red class is therefore not measured. The paper gives the measured share of the red class and states that its cause was not measured. The code of the check (`prepare_reflat`, `reflat_case`, `reflat_summary`) is in the repository with its tests and has produced no row.

## Cost

Timed on the device, one process, the cold first curve (28.7 s, kernel compilation) reported apart: 0.32 s a curve with its 96 shuffles (white, five curves, 19,740 points, period grid 0.5 to 14 d). N is 2,000 curves a class (the Wilson interval of 1/49 at that size is 1.5 to 2.8 percent), M is 1,500 injections a class (50 a cell), and the real stars take 12 curves of 199 shuffles. About 6,000 null curves and 4,500 injections at 0.32 s are 56 minutes, with the red and variable classes' generation and flattening on top (not timed yet); the planets add the batman model of each. The estimate is checked against the measured time of the first class and a miss of more than a factor of two is written down.
